Taxes account.tax

The figure five worksheets have been deferring. A line carries some taxes, each tax a percentage, and the document's Tax is what they add — so this bundle is the one that makes an invoice's Tax a real number instead of a figure copied from the tenant. It brings Odoo's 35 Malaysian SST taxes, ten of them active, and then goes back to the four worksheets that left a hole for it: Products gains Sales and Purchase Taxes, Chart of Accounts gains Default Taxes, and Invoice Lines gains Taxes and Total.

Worth knowing before you review

Odoo 19.4 has moved this form since the source we read

Reading the Odoo 19.0 source alone would have produced the wrong form. On the tenant, Description has moved up into the header and Tax Scope and Active down into Advanced Options, and three installed modules extend the form that a source read never shows: account_tax_python's Formula, MyInvois' Malaysian Tax Type and account_edi_ubl_cii's Tax Category Code. The layout here follows the tenant's own stored view. The one thing that needs your eye is uniqueness: Odoo's constraint is a five-way key and it refuses a clash, where HAP can only catch one after the save — the same ceiling the owner accepted for States.

That last line is the point of the bundle. No per-line tax figure was invented: Odoo has no tax column on a journal item either, and Σ Total − Σ Subtotal is the same number to the cent, because each line's Total is itself rounded to two decimals and this company rounds per line.

Left out on purpose

The tax distribution (invoice and refund repartition lines, and their Tax Grids) — all 36 lines on the tenant are the default, so nothing varies. Fiscal positions and Replaces, which are a model of their own. Groups of taxes — the option stays in the dropdown because Odoo has it, but no tax on this tenant is one. Tax exigibility and its cash-basis account, which Odoo itself hides here because the company has cash basis off. Analytic cost, Malaysian Tax Type, Tax Category Code and the UBL exemption reasons — localisation and e-invoicing, as Contacts left SST and TTx. And a default tax on a new product, which Odoo takes from a company setting rather than a record.

That roles row needed no ruling, where Countries and States each needed one: Odoo's access file gives account.tax exactly what it gives account.account — the accounting administrator writes, everybody else reads. Odoo's menu is stricter, but every internal user can read a tax, which is what lets an invoice line show one.

The three questions the requirements left open

  1. 1
    A roll-up over a multi-relation does follow the relation — and a formula can read itMeasured

    The worry was that HAP's roll-up holds only saved rows, as it does over a subtable. It does not here: a line with no tax reads rate 0 and total 100.00; add a 3 % tax and it reads 3.0 and 103.00. So Total is a plain formula and the workflow fallback was never needed.

  2. 2
    The roll-up's filter takes on a dropdown of the related worksheetMeasured

    A Fixed 7 tax added nothing to the rate, alone or beside the 3 %. That matters: a fixed or custom-formula tax needs different arithmetic, and the filter makes it contribute nothing rather than something wrong.

  3. 3
    The tax fill fits inside the existing account automationMeasured

    Four of its seven paths gained one field each on their update step. No node, gateway or condition was added, moved or re-conditioned — so setting a product on a line now gives the line both its account and the product's taxes, and a line with no product still takes the journal's default account and no tax.

Tests and differences

Twenty-three checks in the browser

Nineteen pass. The worksheet sits where it should; the list carries Odoo's six columns in the tenant's own order with the ten active taxes ticked; the form reads in the specified order with Amount at Odoo's four decimals and the ten tax-group names in the dropdown; the visibility rules all fire, and an archived Custom Formula tax shows its quantity * 0.01 exactly as Odoo does. Products carry the right taxes, the Sales Taxes picker offers only sales taxes, and Chart of Accounts has Default Taxes and nothing in them.

The arithmetic was proved end to end. A line's Taxes drive its rate, the rate drives its Total, and the Total drives all four amounts on the invoice: putting 10% G on a 10.00 line moved its invoice from untaxed 110.00, tax —, total 110.00 to 110.00 / 1.00 / 111.00, and taking it off moved it back. The three documents the earlier worksheets were built from now land on the tenant's own figures to the cent — 10,460.32, 1,143.20 and 14,400.00.

The duplicate check was run as a person would run it. A second tax typed into the form with a name, type, scope and country already taken saved — HAP cannot refuse it — and within fifteen seconds had lost its key, gained a ticked Duplicate name, and appeared in the Duplicate names view, while the original kept its key and its clear box.

Three things were fixed during the test, and re-checked the same day. The five dropdowns drew in the table as progress-bar sliders rather than text, because of one display setting that means something different on a dropdown than on a relation. The Invoice Lines tab had gained Total but not Taxes, so a line showed its tax-inclusive amount with no way to see or change the tax behind it — Odoo's own tab has that column, and it is now there. And the note at the top of the Invoice Lines tab still said the four amounts were figures copied from the tenant and that "taxes themselves need the Taxes bundle", which had stopped being true twice over.

One test could not be isolated: setting a tax from a line's own panel inside an invoice. Before the column fix the field was not on that panel at all, and afterwards the browser could not be steered to it twice running. The arithmetic it would prove was driven through the command line instead. It is worth a reviewer's minute.

Differences from Odoo

  1. 1
    A duplicate name is marked, not refusedHAP behaviour

    Odoo raises "Tax names must be unique!" and the record never exists. Here it saves, then loses its key, gains a tick and sits in the Duplicate names view until someone mends it. HAP's uniqueness is one field where Odoo's key is five, and a workflow runs after the save, never before it. The owner accepted this ceiling for States.

  2. 2
    A product's tax picker offers archived taxesOpen for you

    Odoo's product field filters them out; ours filters on Tax Type alone, because that is what the requirements named. One more condition would close it — but the same behaviour is right on an invoice line, where Odoo deliberately offers archived taxes. Your call.

  3. 3
    A document with no taxed lines reads 0.00 rather than blankHAP behaviour

    The roll-up always writes the figure. Odoo leaves the tax line off entirely.

  4. 4
    Only a percentage tax contributesScope

    A fixed, tax-included or custom-formula tax on a line adds nothing to the rate — three different formulas cannot come out of one sum. Every active tax on this tenant is a percentage, so nothing real is lost.

  5. 5
    Tax Group is a dropdown, not a relationScope

    Odoo's Tax Groups menu is behind developer mode, and its ten groups differ only in the name — same country, same sequence, same payable and receivable accounts. If the model is ever wanted, the dropdown becomes a relation the way a contact's Country and State did.
