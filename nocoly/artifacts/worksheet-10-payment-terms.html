Payment Terms account.payment.term

When an invoice falls due — "30 Days", "End of Following Month", "30% Now, Balance 60 Days". The tenant's ten terms arrive with their lines, and the text stand-in on Invoices is replaced by the real thing: the values were carried across, read back, and the text field deleted. An invoice now takes its term from the customer or vendor, and its Due Date is worked out from the term, as Odoo does.

Worth knowing before you review

Two of Odoo's checks are built but switched off

Odoo refuses a term whose percentages do not add up to 100, and an early discount spread over more than one line. Both were built here on a roll-up of the Due Terms table — and a HAP roll-up only counts the rows that are already saved. On a new term it reads empty, so the rule refused every term, including one with Odoo's own default 100 % line. Both rules are therefore disabled, and nothing checks the percentages today. They stay in place for the day HAP's roll-ups follow the rows being typed. Everything else — the per-line checks, the discount checks, the dates — holds. One thing to try yourself: give a term two lines of 30 % and 30 % and save it; then open an invoice on that term and see which date it gets.

The term follows the customer, as Odoo's compute does: a customer document takes the contact's Customer Payment Terms, a vendor document its Vendor Payment Terms, a journal entry none — and a contact with no term of its own leaves the invoice's alone. The Due Date follows the term: the reference date is the Invoice Date, or the Accounting Date, or today, and each line gives a date — the latest of them wins, with the end-of-month arithmetic and the clamp Odoo uses (the 31st of November is the 30th). Confirm dates the document too, because posting fills an empty Invoice Date.

Roles: Payment Terms and its lines are written by the Accounting Administrator alone, as Odoo's access list has it; the other three roles read them. Nothing is hidden per field — Odoo shows a contact's payment terms to every accounting group.

Differences from Odoo

  1. 1
    Nothing checks that the percentages add up to 100HAP behaviour

    Nor that an early discount keeps to one line. Both rules are built and disabled; a roll-up cannot see the rows being typed.

  2. 2
    No preview of the installmentsScope

    Odoo shows an example amount on an example date with each installment. A formula here cannot walk the lines — but a real invoice's Due Date is the same arithmetic, and it is built.

  3. 3
    Pickers list the newest term firstHAP behaviour

    Odoo orders them by sequence.

  4. 4
    The Lines table shows every line's day-of-monthHAP behaviour

    A rule hides a field on a form, not in a table column. Inside a term's own Due Terms table it is hidden as Odoo hides it.

  5. 5
    An invoice is dated as soon as it has a termNote

    Odoo waits until the invoice has lines.

  6. 6
    The term and the date arrive with the saveHAP behaviour

    Odoo fills them the moment the customer is picked; here the workflows run just after the save.

  7. 7
    A term an invoice uses can still be deletedHAP behaviour

    Odoo refuses and tells you to archive it instead. Only the Accounting Administrator can delete, and Archive is the way to retire a term.

  8. 8
    Sequence is typed, not draggedHAP behaviour

    And a new line does not start at 100 minus the other percentages, nor at the previous line's days plus 30.
