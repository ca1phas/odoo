Nine months at forty hours a week

Calibrated against the build you already did, not invented. Decoding the creation timestamps inside the live worksheet IDs shows the existing Sales app went up over seven days — twenty-eight worksheets on day one alone — and reached about 88% parity. Two phases distort the total. Website at 9 weeks is a quarter of the plan and is not worksheet work — Nocoly is a database, not a site builder, so eCommerce and public pages mean a separate front end talking to HAP over the v3 API. Treat it as its own project. Inventory & Manufacturing at 7 weeks is genuine: 45 worksheets and the 27-control bridge back into Sales.

Drop both and the working ERP — foundation, CRM, Sales, POS, Services, HR, Finance, Marketing — lands in about 21 weeks, roughly five months.

What the rate assumes. The 40 h/week is your input — and it is the rate you have already demonstrated: the existing Sales app took roughly 100 hours across seven days, which is well above forty a week. The productivity inside it is yours; the productivity inside it is the one you demonstrated — several background agents in parallel, Odoo open beside you as the reference, results read back rather than trusted. A person working the HAP form designer alone would be several times slower. The estimate carries no allowance for rebuilding a worksheet you got wrong, which is exactly what the dependency ordering in this plan exists to prevent.

Milestones worth naming

  • Week 1 — core foundation standing. Seven worksheets, no app yet, but every later phase now has something to relate into.
  • Week 5 — CRM live. First complete app: pipeline, views, roles, rules, a won-to-quotation workflow, and a click-through pass behind it.
  • Week 6 — quote to cash. Orders, Order Lines, Invoices, Payments, quotation PDFs. The point the system starts replacing something.
  • Week 16 — the physical business. Inventory, Purchase and Manufacturing in; delivery-driven invoicing real rather than modelled.
  • Week 28 — everything except the public web.

Throughout

Four rules that hold in every phase

01

A worksheet is finished when its relations are wired

Create the worksheet, add its Relation controls, read them back, then move to the next number. Half-wired tables left for a later pass are how a foundation drifts out of dependency order — and HAP will happily let you point a Relation at a worksheet that doesn't exist yet.

02

Relations point down, never sideways

An app worksheet relates into phase 1 freely. It relates into another app's worksheet only through a named bridge pack — a documented set of controls appended to one side, built after both apps stand. Odoo enforces this with separate modules; here it is a review rule.

03

Bridges are their own deliverable

Nocoly has no auto_install, so the trigger is a checklist. One file per pair — state/bridge_sale_stock.json — naming the controls it appends, the workflows it owns and the invariants it must not break. Never smuggled into either app's build.

04

Every phase ends on a seeded, readable-back check

Seed each new worksheet from the matching Odoo tenant and read it back before declaring the phase done. Several HAP writes return success and store nothing; a phase that was never read back is a phase you will re-debug three phases later.

Worksheet counts are persistent models defined by each module at ca1phas/odoo 7bbce824 (19.0 Community), excluding transient wizards, abstract mixins, and ir.*/report.*/theme.* infrastructure. Models that HAP provides natively — users, groups, messages, followers, attachments, model metadata — are excluded from phase 1. Group membership follows odoo.com/page/all-apps; Enterprise-only apps in each group are not counted.
